# Canvas Design

Create social graphics, posters, and covers — text in, PNG/PDF out.

## The Critical Understanding

User instructions are a foundation, not a blueprint. The output is not a literal transcription of the brief — it is a **visual philosophy**: an aesthetic movement expressed through form, color, space, and composition. Information lives in design, not paragraphs.

Input: user text/brief
Output: a design philosophy (markdown) → a single-page PNG or PDF canvas

---

## Phase 1: Design Philosophy Creation

Before touching a canvas, articulate the visual philosophy in a short markdown document.

### How to Generate a Visual Philosophy

1. **Name the movement** (1–2 words): a label for the aesthetic direction
2. **Write 4–6 paragraphs** covering:
   - Space and breathing room
   - Form and geometry
   - Color and its role as information
   - Scale and hierarchy
   - Rhythm and repetition
   - Composition and tension

The philosophy should feel like a design manifesto — opinionated, specific, and meticulously considered. Leave creative room for execution; don't over-specify every element.

Emphasize: the work must appear **expert-level**, labored over, and museum-quality. Craftsmanship should be evident throughout.

### Philosophy Examples

**Concrete Poetry** — Monumental form and bold geometry. Communication happens through massive color blocks and sculptural typography. Ideas are expressed through spatial tension rather than explanatory text.

**Chromatic Language** — Color as the primary information system. Geometric precision where different color zones create meaning. Typography remains minimal; chromatic fields carry the message.

**Analog Meditation** — Quiet visual contemplation through texture and breathing room. Photography and illustration dominate. Vast negative space with restrained typography appearing sparingly.

**Organic Systems** — Natural clustering and modular growth patterns. Rounded forms and organic arrangements. Information communicates through visual diagrams and spatial relationships, not text blocks.

**Geometric Silence** — Pure order and restraint. Grid-based precision and dramatic negative space. Structure conveys meaning through careful alignment and strategic stark graphics.

### Deducing the Subtle Reference

Every piece should contain a niche conceptual thread embedded invisibly in the composition. The topic is a subtle reference — not always literal, always sophisticated. Familiar audiences should intuitively recognize it; others experience an abstract composition.

The reference must remain refined: it enhances depth without self-announcement. Like a jazz musician quoting another song — only those who know will catch it.

---

## Phase 2: Canvas Creation

Execute the design philosophy as a single-page PDF or PNG.

### Requirements

- **Museum-quality output**: the result should appear professionally crafted and worthy of display
- **Text is minimal**: positioned subtly, never dominating the composition
- **All elements contained**: proper margins, nothing bleeding outside boundaries
- **Visual communication first**: let form, color, and composition carry meaning

### Refinement (Final Step)

After the initial composition, refine rather than add:
- Prioritize cohesion and minimalism over decoration
- Remove anything that doesn't serve the visual philosophy
- Achieve a sense of "masterpiece of craftsmanship" — every element intentional

### Multi-Page Option

For projects requiring multiple pages, each page should:
- Follow the same visual philosophy
- Be distinctively different in composition from the others
- Maintain visual storytelling continuity across the set
- Exercise creative freedom within the established aesthetic

---

## Essential Principles

- Visual philosophy over lengthy explanatory text
- Spatial expression and artistic freedom over rigid templates
- Expert craftsmanship that appears labored over
- The subtle reference enriches; it does not explain itself
- Cohesion and restraint beat complexity and decoration
